Getting started

Adding an email account

In the sidebar, choose Add account. Enter your email address and password, then Continue: SimplyMail looks up your provider's settings itself. If it can't find them, or you'd rather, choose Enter settings myself and fill in the incoming server (IMAP or POP3) and the outgoing (SMTP) server, with their ports and security.

Your password is kept in your keyring on Linux, or Windows Credential Manager on Windows, never in a file. Add as many accounts as you like; switch between them under Accounts in the sidebar.

Gmail and app passwords

Gmail doesn't take your usual Google password from mail apps. Create an app password in your Google account (Security, then 2-Step Verification, then App passwords) and use that as the password in SimplyMail.

If you change your Google password, Google may cancel your app passwords: make a new one, and enter it with Settings, Password, Change.

Changing an account's password

If you've changed your password with your provider, open Settings and choose Change beside Password. SimplyMail checks the new one with your servers before keeping it. Nothing else about the account changes.

Removing an account

Pick the account in the sidebar and choose Remove this account at the bottom. SimplyMail forgets it on this computer, with its password, calendar, contacts, tasks, signature, rules and scheduled emails. Your email stays on the server.

Opening SimplyMail when you log in

In Settings, turn on Open SimplyMail when you log in. It opens minimised in the dock on Linux, or the taskbar on Windows, so new-mail notifications and appointment reminders come without you opening it.

Reading

The email list

The middle of the window lists the open folder's emails, newest first; unread ones are in bold. Click one to read it. Scrolling to the end loads older emails.

A paperclip beside an email means it has files attached.

Show unread shows only unread emails, the star beside it only starred ones, and the paperclip only emails with attachments. Choose it again to show all. Refresh (or F5) checks for new mail.

Drag the lines between the sidebar, the list and the email to make them wider or narrower; double-click a line to put it back.

An email in its own window

Double-click an email in the list, or choose New window above it, to open it in a window of its own: to read two emails side by side, or keep one in view while you write.

Everything above an email works there too: reply, forward, print, save, move or delete. Moving or deleting it closes its window.

Conversations

An email and its replies show as one line, under the newest, with how many there are and an arrow that opens them. Your own replies, from your Sent folder, show in it too, as You.

Ticking a conversation ticks all its emails, so Delete, Archive, Move and Junk act on the whole conversation. To list every email on its own, turn off Group conversations in Settings.

All inboxes

With more than one account, All inboxes at the top of the folders lists every account's inbox together, newest first. Each email says which account it came to, and replying, deleting and the rest go through that account.

Pictures in emails

Pictures on the web are hidden at first, as loading them can tell the sender you've opened the email. Choose Show pictures above the email to see them. Pictures sent in the email itself always show.

Attachments

An email's attachments are listed above its text. Click one to open it in the app your computer uses for it, or choose Save beside it to keep it.

Program files (like .exe or .sh) are never opened, as opening one runs it; save it instead if you trust the sender.

Meeting invitations

An email with a meeting invitation shows the meeting, with Accept, Tentative and Decline. Your answer goes in your calendar, and the organiser is told. Join meeting opens an online meeting's link.

Read receipts

When an email asks to be told you've opened it, SimplyMail asks you first: choose Send receipt or Don't send. Nothing is sent unless you choose. Emails in your Junk folder never ask, so spammers can't learn your address works.

Printing and saving an email

Print (or Ctrl+P) opens the email in your browser, ready to print or to save as a PDF.

Save keeps the email as an .eml file, exactly as it came, with its attachments. Most mail apps can open one.

Writing

Writing an email

Choose New email in the sidebar (or Ctrl+N). Each email opens in its own window, so you can keep reading meanwhile. As you type in To, Cc or Bcc, your contacts are suggested. Send it with Send (or Ctrl+Enter).

Reply, Reply all and Forward are above an email you're reading (Ctrl+R, Ctrl+Shift+R, Ctrl+Shift+F).

Attaching files

Choose Attach in the email's window, or drag files from your file manager onto it.

If your email says something is attached ("attached", "attachment", "enclosed") but nothing is, SimplyMail asks before it goes: attach the files, send it anyway, or keep writing. Words in the signature, in the email you're replying to and in a reply's subject don't count.

Spelling

As you write the subject and the email, misspelt words get a red wavy line. Right-click one for the dictionary's suggestions, and choose one to put it in, or choose Add to dictionary so the word (a name, say) is never marked again.

Email addresses, links, words with numbers, words in capitals and lines quoted with ">" aren't checked.

In Settings, choose South African, British or American English, take out words you added, or turn the spell check off. A long subject wraps onto a second line rather than scrolling, so its lines stay under the words.

Signatures

Each account can have a signature, added under new emails, replies and forwards. Set it in Settings, while the account is shown in the sidebar. You can still change it in each email.

Sending from another address

If your provider gives the account other addresses (aliases), add them in Settings, From addresses, while the account is shown in the sidebar: each with the name shown beside it and its own signature.

Then choose From at the top of an email to send from one; its signature takes the place of the other. A reply goes from the address the email came to, and Reply all leaves that address out. Your provider must allow sending from it, or the email is refused.

Drafts

Save draft (or Ctrl+S) keeps an unfinished email in your Drafts folder, where you can finish it here or on your phone; open it and choose Edit draft. SimplyMail also saves a draft every minute while you write, so nothing's lost if it or the computer closes.

Closing SimplyMail while you're writing asks whether to save your emails to Drafts first.

Undo send

After you press Send, the email waits a few seconds before it goes, with Undo and Send now at the bottom of the main window. Undo brings the email back to change it. Choose how long it waits (or turn it off) under Undo send in Settings.

Sending later

Choose Send later in the email's window, then later today, tomorrow morning, Monday morning, or your own date and time. The email waits on this computer, under Scheduled below the folders, where you can send it now, edit it or cancel it.

As no server sends it for you, it goes while SimplyMail is open, or when it next starts.

Templates

To keep an email to start from again, choose Templates, then Save as template. Later, choose Templates and pick it to fill the email you're writing: its recipients and subject, and its text above yours. Your signature and attachments aren't kept. See your templates, and delete them, under Email templates in Settings.

Quick replies

Quick reply, beside Forward above an email you're reading, lists your templates. Choosing one opens a reply to the sender with the template's text already written, above your signature; check it and choose Send.

Only the template's text is used: the reply keeps its own recipient and subject. To make a quick reply, write the text in a new email and choose Templates, then Save as template. Quick reply shows once you have a template with text.

Reminder if no reply

Under the subject, "Remind me if no reply" can be set to 1 day, 3 days or 1 week before you send. If nobody has answered by then, a notification says so, and the email is starred in Sent so it's easy to find and follow up.

A reply counts wherever it is now, even if it was filed or deleted; your own emails in Sent and Drafts don't. Reminders are kept on this computer and come while SimplyMail is open, or when it next starts.

Asking for a read receipt

Tick Ask for a read receipt under the subject. The recipient's mail app may ask them first, or not send one at all.

Organising

Deleting emails

Delete (or the Delete key) moves emails to your Trash folder, where you can get them back. In Trash itself, or with Shift+Delete, emails are deleted for good, after asking.

To delete everything in Trash or Junk, open it and choose Empty, or right-click it in the sidebar.

Ticking several emails

Tick the boxes beside emails to work on them together: the top of the list then offers Archive, Move and Delete for all of them. Escape unticks them all.

Moving and archiving

Archive moves the email to your Archive folder, when you have one. Move picks any folder. You can also drag emails onto a folder in the sidebar; the ticked ones go together.

Junk and spam

The Junk (or Spam) button moves an email to your junk folder, which also teaches servers that learn from it, like Gmail's. In that folder the button says Not junk, and moves it back to the inbox.

If your server has two such folders, right-click the one you want and choose Use for junk.

Stars and unread

Click the star beside an email (or press S) to star it, as Gmail and other mail apps do too. Mark unread keeps an email bold until you deal with it. Each folder in the sidebar shows how many unread emails it has.

Folders

Make a folder with the + beside Folders. Right-click one of your own folders to rename or delete it. The server's own folders, like Inbox, Sent and Drafts, stay as they are.

Marking a folder read

Right-click a folder and choose Mark all as read to mark every email in it read, on the server, including older ones not yet in the list. Its count of unread emails goes. It's offered while the folder has unread emails.

Searching

Type in the search box above the list and press Enter (Ctrl+F puts you there). It finds emails by who they're from or to, or their subject.

Turn on All folders to search every folder at once; each result says which folder it's in. To move or delete one from another folder, open it and choose Go to folder.

Rules for new mail

Settings, Rules for new mail, sorts new emails as they arrive: if who they're from or to, or their subject, contains some words, move them to a folder, mark them read or star them. Run on Inbox now applies them to the emails already there.

Rules run while SimplyMail is open on this computer.

Snoozing an email

To deal with an email later, open it and choose Snooze, then later today, tomorrow morning, Monday morning, or a day and time of your own. It moves to a Snoozed folder (made the first time) and comes back to the folder it was in, unread, at that time.

Snoozing is kept on this computer, so emails come back while SimplyMail is open, or when it next starts. To bring one back sooner, choose Bring back now in the Waiting list, or move it out of the Snoozed folder yourself.

The Waiting list

Waiting, under the folders, lists what this computer is keeping for the account: snoozed emails, with when each comes back, and sent emails waiting for a reply, with when you'll be reminded. It shows once there's something on it.

Bring back now returns a snoozed email to its folder, unread, at once. Stop waiting cancels a reminder.

Unsubscribing from newsletters

When a newsletter or mailing list says how to leave it, Unsubscribe shows beside its sender. SimplyMail asks first, then uses the best way the list offers: telling its website with one click (nothing else is sent), sending an unsubscribe email from your address, or opening the list's own page in your browser to finish there.

It isn't offered in Junk: answering spam only tells the sender your address works, so use Block for those instead. Lists can take a few days to stop.

Blocking a sender

To stop someone's emails reaching your inbox, open one and choose Block beside their address. Their emails go to Junk (or Spam) from then on, and those in the open folder go there at once.

Blocking makes a rule, so it works while SimplyMail is open on this computer. To unblock someone, delete their "Blocked" line in Settings, Rules for new mail.

Saving and importing folders

Right-click a folder and choose Save as .mbox to keep every email in it in one file, which Thunderbird and most mail apps can import. Import emails puts .eml or .mbox files back into a folder, with their dates.

Vacation reply

Settings, Vacation reply, sets an out-of-office answer, with optional first and last days. Your mail server sends it, even with this computer off, at most once a day to each sender. Gmail keeps its own, in Gmail's settings.

Calendar, contacts and tasks

Calendar

Choose Calendar in the sidebar. To see a calendar kept on a server, choose Connect and give the calendar server's address, or, for Gmail, sign in with Google. New appointment adds one, with a time, place, reminder and repeats; you can email the invitation to the people coming. Reminders pop up while SimplyMail is open.

Month, Week and Day, at the top, switch between a month at a glance and a week or a single day with appointments laid out by time of day; the arrows move a month, a week or a day. In Week and Day, all-day appointments sit along the top, ones at the same time sit side by side, and a red line shows the time now.

Print, at the top, prints the month, week or day shown: it opens in your browser, whose print dialog also offers Save as PDF.

To add one at a time, double-click that time in Week or Day: a new appointment opens starting on that half hour.

To move an appointment, drag it in Week or Day to another time (or, in Week, another day); it lands on the quarter hour and keeps its length. To make it longer or shorter, drag its bottom edge instead. A repeating one asks whether to move just that one or the whole series, and one with people coming asks whether to tell them. Appointments you can't change (someone else's invitation, say) don't move.

Contacts

Choose Contacts in the sidebar. Connect an address book on a server, or sign in with Google for Gmail. New contact adds someone, and Write email starts an email to them. From an email, Add to contacts adds its sender.

Contact groups

To write to the same people often, make a group: in Contacts, choose Groups, then New group. Name it, tick the contacts in it, and type any other addresses.

Then type the group's name in To, Cc or Bcc and choose it to add everyone, or choose Write beside it in Groups. People already in the field aren't added twice. Groups are kept on this computer, for the account shown.

Tasks

Choose Tasks in the sidebar. New task adds one, with a due date. Assign it to someone's email address, and they're emailed the task.

Settings and privacy

Light and dark

Settings, Appearance: Match the computer, Light or Dark.

Server certificates

If a server shows a certificate your computer doesn't trust, SimplyMail asks before carrying on, with the details to check it. Only trust it if you know the server. See and remove certificates you've trusted under Trusted certificates in Settings.

Your privacy

SimplyMail talks to your own mail servers only. Passwords stay in your keyring on Linux, or Windows Credential Manager on Windows; settings, templates, rules and scheduled emails stay in files only you can read. Pictures on the web wait until you ask for them.

Keyboard shortcuts

These work in the main window while you're not typing in a box (Ctrl ones work anywhere in it):

Ctrl+N: Write a new email
Ctrl+R: Reply
Ctrl+Shift+R: Reply all
Ctrl+Shift+F: Forward
Up / Down, K / J: Read the previous or next email
Enter: Open the email being read in its own window
R / A / F: Reply, reply all or forward
E: Archive the ticked emails, or the one being read
! (Shift+1): Send them to Junk
U: Mark the email being read unread
X: Tick the email being read, or untick it
/: Search the folder, or all inboxes
Delete or # (Shift+3): Move the ticked emails, or the one being read, to Trash
Shift+Delete: Delete them for good, after asking
Ctrl+F: Search the folder, or all inboxes
Ctrl+P: Print the email being read
S: Star the email being read, or take its star off
F5: Refresh
Escape: Untick all
F1: Open the help

In an email you're writing: Ctrl+Enter sends, Ctrl+S saves a draft.

When something's wrong

The server refused the password

Check the password with your provider. For Gmail, use an app password (see Gmail and app passwords). Once it's right, enter it with Settings, Password, Change.

No new-mail notifications

On Linux: Notifications come through your desktop, while SimplyMail is open. They need the notify-send program, from the libnotify-bin package; install it if they don't appear.

On Windows: Notifications come from SimplyMail's icon in the taskbar's corner, while SimplyMail is open. Check that Windows' notification settings allow them.

POP3 accounts

POP3 accounts have only an inbox: no folders, stars, drafts, rules or vacation reply, and search only looks through the emails already loaded. If your provider offers IMAP, add the account again choosing IMAP for all of these.
